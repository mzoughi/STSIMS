Physics Guide

Concepts, equations & further reading

What is this simulation?

This interactive 3D lab shows how a charged particle moves as it enters and exits a region of uniform magnetic field bounded between $x = 0$ and $x = 6\text{ m}$.

Magnetic forces do zero work, meaning they only deflect the particle's direction without changing its speed.

  • Straight Line — $\vec{v} \parallel \vec{B}$; zero Lorentz force $\vec{F} = 0$.
  • Circular Arc — $\vec{v} \perp \vec{B}$; particle curves along a circular path inside the field.
  • Helical Motion — $\vec{v}$ angled relative to $\vec{B}$; spiraling motion along the field direction.

Two field extents

Bounded Box mode confines $\vec{B}$ to $x \in [0,6]$, so you see a single entry → curve → exit event — useful for studying deflection at a boundary.

Uniform mode fills all space with $\vec{B}$, so nothing ever turns the field off: the particle just keeps gyrating on the same closed loop forever. Viewed from an angle, a circular orbit projects as an ellipse — try the "Bound Circular Orbit" preset to see it.

The Lorentz Force

A charge $q$ moving with velocity $\vec{v}$ through magnetic field $\vec{B}$ experiences:

$$\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$

This is a force, not an acceleration — Newton's second law converts it via the particle's mass $m$:

$$\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m} = \frac{q}{m}(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$$

Inside the field region, the circular arc radius and cyclotron period are:

$$r = \frac{m v_\perp}{|q| |\vec{B}|} \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q||\vec{B}|}$$

Notice the period is independent of speed — a heavier particle (or weaker field/charge) always takes longer to complete a loop, regardless of how fast it's moving.

Why the orbit doesn't spiral

This sim integrates motion with the Boris algorithm, the standard method used in plasma physics for magnetic-only motion. Each step is an exact rotation of $\vec{v}$, so speed — and therefore the orbit radius — never drifts, even after thousands of loops. A simpler method (plain Euler) would slowly leak energy into the orbit and spiral it outward instead of tracing a clean, closed curve.
